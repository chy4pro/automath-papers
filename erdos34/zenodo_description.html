For a permutation a of {1,…,n}, let S(a) be the set of distinct nonempty linear consecutive sums, and let f(n) = maxa |S(a)|. This paper proves f(n) ≤ (4435834/107)n2 + 1100n for every integer n ≥ 1. Hence f(n) ≤ 0.4436n2 for every n ≥ 66,265,061.

At length 3p, for every prime p ≥ 216, an explicit complementary-triple family has average and maximum at least (803/288)p2 − 50000p7/4(log p + 3)8 over nonzero multipliers and the stated restricted translation interval. The primitive-root ordering retains the final zero field label. Logarithms are natural. The lower expression becomes positive only when p > exp(u*), where u* > 29 is the unique solution of 50000 exp(−u*/4)(u*+3)8 = 803/288; u*/log 2 is approximately 304.220. Thus 216 is a technical side condition, not an onset of numerical content. The construction gives at least 0.309799(3p)2 distinct sums for every prime p ≥ 2416.

Using precisely Dusart's prime-interval theorem (1998 thesis, Théorème 1.9, printed p.35), which supplies a prime in (x, x(1 + 1/(2 log2x))] for every real x ≥ 3275, padding gives f(n) ≥ 0.309799n2 for every integer n ≥ 24096. Consequently 0.309799 ≤ liminf f(n)/n2 ≤ limsup f(n)/n2 ≤ 0.4435834. The upper proof uses coupled rearrangement inequalities and an exact integer certificate; the lower proof uses Fourier completion, restricted translations and restoration of overlaps already covered by a base set. All errors and sufficient onsets are explicit. The construction is specified by finite maximization; no efficient algorithm at these onsets is claimed.

Literature. Both bounds improve the strongest coefficients we located in a bounded literature search completed on 12 October 2026, namely 3/2 − 2/√e and 1/4 + π/16 in Jakub Konieczny, On consecutive sums in permutations, Journal of Combinatorics 12 (2021), 413–477, Theorem 1.2 (2015 preprint). The search was not exhaustive and does not establish priority. Hegyvári's earlier quadratic-residue construction gives coefficient 1/18, as explained in Konieczny §1.5; primitive-root fixed-lag injectivity is also a classical ingredient. The original o(n2) assertion in Erdős Problem 34 was already disproved. The bounds 0.44 above and 0.31 below, and the existence and value of a limit, are not established here.

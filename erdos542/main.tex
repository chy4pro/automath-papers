\begin{abstract}
For a positive integer $n$ let $\rho(n)$ be the maximum of
$\sum_{a\in A}1/a$ over all sets $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ such that
$\lcm(a,b)>n$ for all distinct $a,b\in A$. We prove that
$\rho(n)\leq 1.0087578638+87.5686331624/n$ for every $n\geq1$. In
particular $\rho(n)\leq1.0170$ for $n\geq10625$ and $\rho(n)\leq1.01$ for
$n\geq70499$. As $n\to\infty$ this improves the bound of Chen (1996),
whose leading constant is $1.0170166$; that bound is the strongest we
located in a literature search of 12 October 2026. For
$70\leq n\leq8871$ Chen's explicit bound remains smaller. The proof uses
weak linear-programming duality, with dual weights that depend on $m/n$
and on $\gcd(m,6)$. A certificate of $67$ dyadic rationals reduces dual
feasibility, for all $n$ at once, to $239\,980$ finite inequalities and
four tail inequalities. A short Python program checks all of them in
exact integer and rational arithmetic. The conjecture
$\rho(n)\leq1+o(1)$ remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Let $n$ be a positive integer. We call a set $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$
\emph{lcm-separated} if $\lcm(a,b)>n$ for all distinct $a,b\in A$, and we
put
\[
 \rho(n)=\max\Bigl\{\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a:\ A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
 \text{ lcm-separated}\Bigr\}.
\]
Every singleton is lcm-separated, so $\rho(n)\geq1$; the set $\{1\}$ is
allowed. Erd\H{o}s asked whether $\rho(n)\leq31/30$ for all $n$. This is
part of Problem~542 in Bloom's list \cite{EP542}; Section~\ref{sec:related}
recalls what is known. Our main result is an explicit bound with leading
constant below $1.0088$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $V_1$ and $E_1$ be the constants defined in \eqref{eq:VE} from the
certificate in Appendix~\ref{app:cert}. They satisfy
\begin{gather*}
 E_1=\frac{564156623381}{6442450944}=87.568633162261297305\ldots,\\
 1.008757863713504319\leq V_1\leq1.008757863713504320 .
\end{gather*}
For every integer $n\geq1$ and every set $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ with
$\lcm(a,b)>n$ for all distinct $a,b\in A$,
\[
 \sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\ \leq\ V_1+\frac{E_1}{n}\ \leq\
 1.0087578638+\frac{87.5686331624}{n}.
\]
In other words, $\rho(n)\leq1.0087578638+87.5686331624/n$ for all
$n\geq1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:onsets}
$\rho(n)\leq1.0170$ for every $n\geq10625$, and $\rho(n)\leq1.01$ for
every $n\geq70499$. These are the least onsets that follow from the
displayed constants $1.0087578638$ and $87.5686331624$.
\end{corollary}

The proof (Sections~\ref{sec:duality} and~\ref{sec:cert}) is weak
duality for the linear-programming relaxation of the problem. If $A$ is
lcm-separated, the sets of multiples $\{m\leq n: a\mid m\}$, $a\in A$, are
pairwise disjoint. So any nonnegative weights $z_1,\dots,z_n$ that put
mass at least $1/a$ on the multiples of each $a\leq n$ give
$\rho(n)\leq\sum_m z_m$. We take $z_m=c_{\gcd(m,6)}(m/n)/m$, where
$c_1,c_2,c_3,c_6$ are step functions on $(1/10,1]$ with $54$ steps.
Because $6$ is squarefree, the dual constraints for all $n$ at once
reduce to inequalities $F_\gamma(x)\geq1$, for real $x\in(0,1]$ and
$\gamma\mid6$, that do not involve $n$. The functions $F_\gamma$ are
piecewise constant. This reduces the range $x\geq10^{-4}$ to $59\,995$
points, and an elementary estimate for harmonic sums in arithmetic
progressions handles $x<10^{-4}$. All resulting inequalities are checked
in exact arithmetic.

The dependence on $\gcd(m,6)$ lets the dual put more weight on integers
with more small divisors. In floating-point experiments on the grids we
tried, weights depending on $m/n$ alone did not get below about $1.0172$.
This observation guided the search; it is not a statement about that
family of weights, and it is not used in the proof.

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives $\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho(n)\leq
1.0087578638$. It does not prove the conjecture $\rho(n)\leq1+o(1)$
\cite{EP542}, and we do not claim that the constant is sharp
(Remark~\ref{rem:small}). The error term $87.5686331624/n$ is explicit;
no unspecified constants occur. The error term is larger than Chen's, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does not improve Chen's explicit bound for every
$n$ (Remark~\ref{rem:chen}).

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
Problem~542 in Bloom's list \cite{EP542} asks whether every set
$A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $\lcm(a,b)>n$ for all distinct $a,b\in A$
satisfies $\sum_{a\in A}1/a\leq31/30$. It also asks a separate question
about the integers $b\leq n$ that divide no element of $A$. The problem
page records both questions as solved. The constant $31/30$ cannot be
lowered, since $\{2,3,5\}$ is lcm-separated for $n=5$. The literature
records $\rho(5)=31/30$ and $\rho(11)=4699/4620=1.01709\ldots$, the
latter attained by $\{3,4,5,7,11\}$ \cite{Ch96,We}.

The problem page and its discussion thread describe $\rho(n)\leq1+o(1)$
as conjectural. They attribute to Erd\H{o}s, Schinzel and Szekeres the
stronger conjecture that $\{2,3,5\}$ and $\{3,4,5,7,11\}$ are the only
such sets with reciprocal sum greater than $1$ \cite{EP542}. The set
$\{2,3,5\}$ is lcm-separated only for $n=5$, and $\{3,4,5,7,11\}$ only for
$n=11$. Since $\{1\}$ is always admissible, this conjecture would give
$\rho(n)=1$ for every $n$ other than $5$ and $11$, in particular for every
$n\geq12$.

Chen \cite[Theorem~2, p.~104]{Ch96} proved that $\rho(n)<1.0170166$ for
all sufficiently large $n$, and explicitly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chen}
 \rho(n)<1.0170166+\frac{14.21}{n-58}\qquad\text{for every integer }n\geq59.
\end{equation}
The same paper notes that $\rho(n)<1.017099<\rho(11)$ for
$n\geq172509$. Its Theorem~1 is a conditional route to $\rho(n)\to1$,
not an unconditional result. Weighted counting over the disjoint sets of
multiples already appears in \cite{Ch96} (Lemma~1 on p.~105, and the
proof of Theorem~1). The duality principle of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} is
therefore not new. The contribution of this note is the family of
weights, which depend on $\gcd(m,6)$, and the exact certificate.

Chen \cite[Theorem~1]{Ch97} also studied a variant $\rho_m(n)$ in which
the elements of $A$ are at most $m$ while their pairwise least common
multiples exceed $n$. For fixed $0<\delta<1-\log2$ and every
$\varepsilon>0$ he proved $|\rho_m(n)-1|<\varepsilon$ for all sufficiently
large $n$ and $n\exp(-(\log n)^\delta)\leq m\leq\varepsilon n$. This
regime, $m=o(n)$, does not include $m=n$. Weingartner
\cite[Theorem~3 and Question~1]{We} considers a variant $R(x)$ in which
the element $1$ is excluded. He proves the lower bound
$R(x)\geq1-\kappa/\log x+O((\log x)^{-3/2})$ with
$0.543595<\kappa<0.543804$, and asks whether this is the asymptotic
value. He does not prove an upper bound of the form $1+o(1)$.

We carried out a bounded literature search on 12 October 2026. It
covered the problem page with its comments and discussion thread, arXiv
and targeted web searches (on the numerical constants, on primitive and
lcm-separated sets, and on linear-programming and flow certificates), the
OpenAlex index and the formal-conjectures repository. We also examined
results on primitive sets with the weight $1/(a\log a)$ and certificate
frameworks for related problems; none of them gives a bound for
$\rho(n)$ of the present kind. We did not locate a bound for $\rho(n)$
with leading constant below $1.0170166$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} therefore
improves the strongest bound we located in this search. The search was
not exhaustive: one further citation index refused our queries (rate
limit) and could not be used, the OpenAlex index did not resolve the
record of \cite{Ch96}, so its forward citations could not be traced
there, and the community chat archive was searched only in part.

\section{Duality and the dual weights}\label{sec:duality}
\begin{lemma}[weak duality]\label{lem:dual}
Let $n\geq1$ and let $z_1,\dots,z_n\geq0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualfeas}
 \sum_{1\leq k\leq n/a}z_{ka}\ \geq\ \frac1a
 \qquad\text{for every }1\leq a\leq n.
\end{equation}
Then $\rho(n)\leq\sum_{m=1}^n z_m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ be lcm-separated and put
$M(a)=\{ka: k\geq1,\ ka\leq n\}$. If $m\in M(a)\cap M(b)$ for distinct
$a,b\in A$, then $\lcm(a,b)\mid m$, so $\lcm(a,b)\leq m\leq n$, which is
impossible. Hence the sets $M(a)$, $a\in A$, are pairwise disjoint, and
by \eqref{eq:dualfeas} and $z\geq0$,
\[
 \sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\leq\sum_{a\in A}\ \sum_{m\in M(a)}z_m
 \leq\sum_{m=1}^n z_m . \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:lp}
If $A$ is lcm-separated, every $m\leq n$ has at most one divisor in $A$.
So the indicator vector of $A$ is feasible for the linear program:
maximize $\sum_{a\leq n}y_a/a$ subject to $\sum_{a\mid m}y_a\leq1$ for
$1\leq m\leq n$ and $y\geq0$. Condition \eqref{eq:dualfeas} with $z\geq0$
describes the feasible points of the dual program, which minimizes
$\sum_m z_m$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} is weak duality for this pair.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Weights depending on \texorpdfstring{$m/n$}{m/n} and on
\texorpdfstring{$\gcd(m,6)$}{gcd(m,6)}}
Let
\[
 G=\{p/j:\ 1\leq p\leq4,\ p\leq j\leq10p\}.
\]
This set has $55$ elements $\tfrac1{10}=g_0<g_1<\dots<g_{54}=1$, listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:cert}. They define the $54$ bins
$I_b=(g_b,g_{b+1}]$, $0\leq b\leq53$. For each divisor $\eta$ of $6$ and
each bin $b$ the certificate fixes a number
\[
 c_{\eta,b}=C_{\eta,b}/2^{30},\qquad C_{\eta,b}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0};
\]
$67$ of the $216$ integers $C_{\eta,b}$ are nonzero
(Appendix~\ref{app:cert}). Define step functions on $(0,1]$ by
\[
 c_\eta(t)=c_{\eta,b}\quad(t\in I_b),\qquad
 c_\eta(t)=0\quad(0<t\leq\tfrac1{10}),
\]
and, for a given $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:z}
 z_m=\frac{c_{\gcd(m,6)}(m/n)}{m}\qquad(1\leq m\leq n).
\end{equation}
For $\gamma\mid6$ and an integer $k$ write
$\gamma\jn k=\lcm(\gamma,\gcd(k,6))$, with $\gcd(0,6)=6$. For real
$0<x\leq1$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 F_\gamma(x)=\sum_{1\leq k\leq1/x}\frac{c_{\gamma\jn k}(kx)}{k}.
\end{equation}
Finally define the constants
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VE}
 V_\gamma=\frac16\sum_{r=0}^{5}\sum_{b=0}^{53}c_{\gamma\jn r,b}
 \log\frac{g_{b+1}}{g_b},
 \qquad
 E_\gamma=\sum_{r=0}^{5}\sum_{b=0}^{53}\frac{c_{\gamma\jn r,b}}{g_b}.
\end{equation}
For $\gamma=1$ these are the constants $V_1$ and $E_1$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\begin{lemma}[reduction to inequalities free of $n$]\label{lem:reduce}
Suppose that $F_\gamma(x)\geq1$ for every $\gamma\mid6$ and every real
$x\in(0,1]$. Then for every $n\geq1$ the weights \eqref{eq:z}, which are nonnegative
because every $C_{\eta,b}\geq0$, satisfy \eqref{eq:dualfeas}, and hence
$\rho(n)\leq\sum_{m\leq n}z_m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $1\leq a\leq n$, $\gamma=\gcd(a,6)$ and $x=a/n$. A prime
$p\in\{2,3\}$ divides $ka$ if and only if it divides $a$ or $k$; since
$6$ is squarefree, $\gcd(ka,6)=\gamma\jn k$. Therefore
\[
 a\sum_{1\leq k\leq n/a}z_{ka}
 =\sum_{1\leq k\leq n/a}\frac{c_{\gamma\jn k}(ka/n)}{k}
 =F_\gamma(x)\geq1 .
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduce} does not involve $n$; this is
why a single certificate covers all $n$. Section~\ref{sec:cert} verifies
it and bounds $\sum_{m\leq n}z_m$.

\section{The certificate and its exact verification}\label{sec:cert}
\subsection{Two elementary lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[harmonic sums in progressions]\label{lem:ap}
Let $L\geq1$ and $r$ be integers and let $0<A<B$ be real. Then
\[
 \frac1L\log\frac{B}{A+L}\ \leq
 \sum_{\substack{A<k\leq B\\ k\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ L)}}\frac1k
 \ \leq\ \frac1A+\frac1L\log\frac BA .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower bound.} If $A+L\geq B$ the left side is at most $0$.
Otherwise every interval $(t,t+L]$ contains exactly one member of the
progression, so the least member $k_0>A$ satisfies $k_0\leq A+L<B$; let
$k_1$ be the largest member $\leq B$, so that $k_1+L>B$. The intervals
$[k,k+L]$, for members $k$ with $k_0\leq k\leq k_1$, have disjoint
interiors and cover $[A+L,B]$. Since
$1/k\geq\frac1L\int_k^{k+L}dt/t$, the sum is at least
$\frac1L\int_{A+L}^{B}dt/t$.

\emph{Upper bound.} If the sum is empty there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise its first term is $1/k_0<1/A$. Every later term satisfies
$1/k\leq\frac1L\int_{k-L}^{k}dt/t$, and these intervals $[k-L,k]$ have
disjoint interiors and lie in $[A,B]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[piecewise constancy]\label{lem:pc}
Let $S=\{p/N:\ 1\leq p\leq4,\ N\geq1\}$. For $x\in(0,1]$ let
$v(x)=\min\{s\in S: s\geq x\}$. Then $F_\gamma(x)=F_\gamma(v(x))$ for
every $\gamma\mid6$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The minimum exists because $S\cap[x,\infty)$ is finite (it lies in
$\{p/N: N\leq4/x\}$) and contains $1$. Write $v=v(x)$. By minimality,
for every $s\in S$ we have $x\leq s$ if and only if $v\leq s$. For each
$k\geq1$, the $k$-th term of \eqref{eq:F} is determined by which of the
following holds: $kx\leq g_0$ (the term is $0$); $g_b<kx\leq g_{b+1}$
for some $0\leq b\leq53$ (the term is $c_{\gamma\jn k,b}/k$); or $kx>1$
(the term is absent). These conditions are Boolean combinations of
conditions $x\leq g_i/k$, and $g_i/k=p'/(jk)\in S$ whenever $g_i=p'/j$
with $p'\leq4$. So $x$ and $v$ satisfy the same conditions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The range \texorpdfstring{$10^{-4}\leq x\leq1$}{1e-4 <= x <= 1}:
an exact integer check}\label{subsec:finite}
Let $x_1=10^{-4}$. Since $x_1\in S$ and $1\in S$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:pc} maps $[x_1,1]$ into $S\cap[x_1,1]$. In lowest terms
the elements of $S\cap[x_1,1]$ are the fractions $v=p/N$ with
$1\leq p\leq4$, $p\leq N\leq10^4p$ and $\gcd(p,N)=1$. There are
$10000+9999+19998+19998=59\,995$ of them, so it suffices to verify the
$4\cdot59\,995=239\,980$ inequalities $F_\gamma(v)\geq1$. For each of
these points $\lfloor N/p\rfloor\leq10^4$.

\emph{Integer enclosure.} Put $Q=2^{48}$ and, for $1\leq k\leq10^4$,
$h_{\gamma,b}(k)=\lfloor2^{18}C_{\gamma\jn k,b}/k\rfloor$, so that
\[
 \frac{h_{\gamma,b}(k)}{Q}\leq\frac{c_{\gamma\jn k,b}}{k}
 <\frac{h_{\gamma,b}(k)+1}{Q}.
\]
For $v=p/N$ let $K_i=\lfloor g_iN/p\rfloor$ $(0\leq i\leq54)$, the
number of $k\geq1$ with $kv\leq g_i$. The $k$ with $kv\in I_b$ are
exactly those with $K_b<k\leq K_{b+1}$, the $k\leq K_0$ contribute $0$,
and $K_{54}=\lfloor N/p\rfloor$ is the cutoff $k\leq1/v$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T_\gamma(v):=\sum_{b=0}^{53}\ \sum_{K_b<k\leq K_{b+1}}h_{\gamma,b}(k)
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 \frac{T_\gamma(v)}{Q}\leq F_\gamma(v)<\frac{T_\gamma(v)+10^4}{Q}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Arithmetic model.} The program \texttt{verify.py} computes
$T_\gamma(v)$ from the prefix sums $\sum_{k\leq K}h_{\gamma,b}(k)$ and
the integers $K_i$, which it obtains by exact integer division. All proof
arithmetic uses Python integers and \texttt{fractions.Fraction}; no
floating-point number enters the proof. The prefix sums are stored for
$K\leq40\,000$ (only $K\leq10^4$ is queried) in arrays of signed
$64$-bit integers. Every stored prefix is at most
\[
 2\,650\,000\,000\cdot2^{18}\cdot H_{40000}
 \leq2\,650\,000\,000\cdot2^{18}\cdot16
 =11\,114\,905\,600\,000\,000<2^{63},
\]
because every $C_{\eta,b}<2\,650\,000\,000$ and
$H_{40000}=\sum_{k\leq40000}1/k\leq16$; for the latter, split
$1,\dots,40000$ into the $16$ blocks $[2^j,2^{j+1})$, $0\leq j\leq15$,
each contributing at most $1$. An out-of-range store would raise an
error rather than wrap around.

\emph{Result.} The minimum of $T_\gamma(v)/Q$ over all $239\,980$ pairs is
\[
 \frac{281475258209621}{2^{48}}=1.000001000085223523\ldots>1,
\]
attained at $(v,\gamma)=(4/15,1)$. By \eqref{eq:T}, $F_\gamma(x)\geq1$
for all $x\in[x_1,1]$ and all $\gamma\mid6$.

\emph{Exact minimum.} The program also evaluates $F_\gamma(v)$ exactly,
as a rational number, for every pair with $T_\gamma(v)/Q\leq F_1(4/15)$.
Only $(4/15,1)$ qualifies; every other pair has
$F_\gamma(v)\geq T_\gamma(v)/Q>F_1(4/15)$. So the minimum over the
finite range is
\[
 F_1(4/15)=\frac{C_{2,48}}{2\cdot2^{30}}+\frac{C_{3,52}}{3\cdot2^{30}}
 =\frac{6442457387}{6442450944}=1.0000010000852247\ldots
\]
Here $k=1,2,3$, with $kv=4/15\in I_{36}$, $8/15\in I_{48}=(1/2,4/7]$
and $4/5\in I_{52}=(3/4,4/5]$; the term $k=1$ vanishes because
$C_{1,36}=0$.

\subsection{The range \texorpdfstring{$0<x<10^{-4}$}{0 < x < 1e-4}}
\label{subsec:tail}
Let $0<x<x_1$ and $\gamma\mid6$. Group the terms of \eqref{eq:F} by the
residue $r$ of $k$ modulo $6$ and by bin. We have $\gamma\jn k=\gamma\jn
r$; moreover $kx\in I_b$ if and only if $g_b/x<k\leq g_{b+1}/x$, and the
terms with $kx\leq g_0$ vanish. Hence
\[
 F_\gamma(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{5}\sum_{b=0}^{53}c_{\gamma\jn r,b}
 \sum_{\substack{g_b/x<k\leq g_{b+1}/x\\ k\equiv r\pmodsix}}\frac1k .
\]
The lower bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:ap} with $L=6$, $A=g_b/x$ and
$B=g_{b+1}/x$, together with $c\geq0$, $\log(1+y)\leq y$ for $y\geq0$,
and $E_\gamma\geq0$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 F_\gamma(x)\geq\sum_{r,b}c_{\gamma\jn r,b}
 \Bigl(\frac16\log\frac{g_{b+1}}{g_b}
 -\frac16\log\Bigl(1+\frac{6x}{g_b}\Bigr)\Bigr)
 \geq V_\gamma-xE_\gamma\geq V_\gamma-x_1E_\gamma .
\end{equation}

\emph{Rational logarithms.} For rational $y>1$ put
$s=(y-1)/(y+1)\in(0,1)$ and $\ell(y)=2\sum_{j=0}^{29}s^{2j+1}/(2j+1)$.
Then
\[
 0\leq\log y-\ell(y)=2\int_0^s\frac{t^{60}}{1-t^2}\,dt
 \leq\frac{2s^{61}}{61(1-s^2)}.
\]
Using these bounds for $\log(g_{b+1}/g_b)$ and $c\geq0$, the program
computes rationals $V_\gamma^-\leq V_\gamma\leq V_\gamma^+$; $E_\gamma$
is rational. It checks $V_\gamma^--x_1E_\gamma-1>0$ for the four values
of $\gamma$ in exact arithmetic. Table~\ref{tab:tail} lists the results,
with decimals rounded outward.

\subsection{Total mass; proofs of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:onsets}}\label{subsec:mass}
Let $n\geq1$. Group the $m\leq n$ by residue $r$ modulo $6$ and by bin:
$m/n\in I_b$ if and only if $ng_b<m\leq ng_{b+1}$, and the terms with
$m/n\leq g_0$ vanish. The upper bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:ap} with $L=6$,
$A=ng_b$ and $B=ng_{b+1}$, which also holds when no member of the
progression lies in $(A,B]$, gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{m=1}^n z_m
 &=\sum_{r=0}^{5}\sum_{b=0}^{53}c_{\gcd(r,6),b}
 \sum_{\substack{ng_b<m\leq ng_{b+1}\\ m\equiv r\pmodsix}}\frac1m\\*
 &\leq\sum_{r=0}^{5}\sum_{b=0}^{53}c_{\gcd(r,6),b}\Bigl(\frac1{ng_b}
 +\frac16\log\frac{g_{b+1}}{g_b}\Bigr)
 =V_1+\frac{E_1}{n},
\end{align*}
because $1\jn r=\gcd(r,6)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Sections~\ref{subsec:finite} and~\ref{subsec:tail} the hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduce} holds, so
$\rho(n)\leq\sum_{m\leq n}z_m\leq V_1+E_1/n$ for every $n\geq1$. In
exact rational arithmetic the program verifies that
$V_1^+\leq10087578638/10^{10}$ and $E_1\leq875686331624/10^{10}$. The
stated values of $V_1$ and $E_1$ are those of Table~\ref{tab:tail}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:onsets}]
The bound $1.0087578638+87.5686331624/n$ decreases in $n$. For
$B\in\{1.0170,\,1.01\}$ the least $n$ with
$1.0087578638+87.5686331624/n\leq B$ is
$\lceil87.5686331624/(B-1.0087578638)\rceil$. Since
$87.5686331624/0.0082421362=10624.50\ldots$ and
$87.5686331624/0.0012421362=70498.41\ldots$, these are $10625$ and
$70499$. The program confirms both values in exact rational arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\emph{How the certificate was found} (not part of the proof). A

\section{Remarks}\label{sec:remarks}
\begin{remark}[small $n$; an observation, not a theorem]\label{rem:small}
An exhaustive computer search gives $\rho(5)=31/30$,
$\rho(11)=4699/4620$, and $\rho(n)=1$ for every other $n\leq29$. In
particular $\rho(n)=1$ for $12\leq n\leq29$, as the conjecture of
Erd\H{o}s, Schinzel and Szekeres predicts. The search was run
independently, with different programs, by the agent that found the
certificate and by both referees (see the section on provenance). It is
not part of the deposit, and nothing in this paper depends on it. In
floating-point computations, the solver returned the optimal value $1.0$
for the linear program of Remark~\ref{rem:lp} at $n=12,\dots,30$ and at
$n=60$, $100$, $200$, $400$, $1000$, $2000$ and $5000$. These
computations are not proofs. If $\rho(n)=1$ for all $n\geq12$, then the
constant $1.0087578638$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is not sharp.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[comparison with Chen's explicit bound]\label{rem:chen}
Put $B(n)=1.0087578638+87.5686331624/n$, and let
$B_C(n)=1.0170166+14.21/(n-58)$ be the bound \eqref{eq:chen}. For
$n>58$, the function $n(n-58)(B(n)-B_C(n))$ is a quadratic polynomial in
$n$ with negative leading coefficient $1.0087578638-1.0170166$. It is
negative at $n=69$ and $n=8872$, and positive at $n=70$ and $n=8871$.
Hence $B(n)<B_C(n)$ for $59\leq n\leq69$ and for all $n\geq8872$, while
$B(n)>B_C(n)$ for $70\leq n\leq8871$. So Theorem~\ref{thm:main} improves
\eqref{eq:chen} for every $n\geq8872$ (for $59\leq n\leq69$ both bounds
exceed $2$), but not for $70\leq n\leq8871$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[a bound for all $n\geq59$]\label{rem:combined}
Combining Corollary~\ref{cor:onsets} with \eqref{eq:chen} gives
$\rho(n)\leq1.0170+15/(n-58)$ for every $n\geq59$. For
$59\leq n\leq47648$ this follows from \eqref{eq:chen}, because
\[
 \frac{0.79}{n-58}\geq\frac{0.79}{47590}=0.0000166+\frac{3}{23795000000};
\]
for $n\geq10625$ it follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:onsets}. This bound
has the same shape as \eqref{eq:chen}, with leading constant $1.0170$
instead of $1.0170166$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[limits of the method]\label{rem:limits}
In \eqref{eq:tail} the tail costs $x_1E_1=0.00875686\ldots$ for
$\gamma=1$, almost the whole excess $V_1-1=0.00875786\ldots$. Lowering
the leading constant within this scheme needs a smaller $x_1$, which
enlarges the finite check, or a sharper tail estimate that uses
cancellation between adjacent bins. We have not pursued either. A proof
of $\rho(n)\leq1+o(1)$ along these lines would need dual weights whose
total mass tends to $1$ uniformly in $n$.
\end{remark}

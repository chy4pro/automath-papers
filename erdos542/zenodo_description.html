For a positive integer n let ρ(n) be the maximum of ∑a∈A 1/a over all sets A ⊆ {1,…,n} such that lcm(a,b) > n for all distinct a, b ∈ A (Erdős Problem #542). We prove that ρ(n) ≤ 1.0087578638 + 87.5686331624/n for every integer n ≥ 1. In particular ρ(n) ≤ 1.0170 for n ≥ 10625 and ρ(n) ≤ 1.01 for n ≥ 70499; these are the least onsets that follow from the displayed constants.

The proof uses weak linear-programming duality. If A is such a set, the sets of multiples of its elements are pairwise disjoint, so nonnegative weights zm that put mass at least 1/a on the multiples of every a ≤ n bound ρ(n) by ∑ zm. We take zm = cgcd(m,6)(m/n)/m with four step functions on (1/10, 1]. Because 6 is squarefree, the dual constraints for all n at once reduce to inequalities that do not involve n. A certificate of 67 dyadic rationals reduces them to 239 980 finite inequalities and four tail inequalities. A short Python program (standard library only, about 2 CPU seconds) checks all of them in exact integer and rational arithmetic. The theorem does not prove the conjecture ρ(n) ≤ 1 + o(1), and the constant 1.0087578638 is not claimed to be sharp.

Literature. As n → ∞ the bound improves the leading constant 1.0170166 of Y.-G. Chen, On a problem of P. Erdős, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 62 (1996), 101–114, Theorem 2. That is the strongest bound we located in a literature search of 12 October 2026. Chen's explicit form 1.0170166 + 14.21/(n − 58), valid for n ≥ 59, remains smaller for 70 ≤ n ≤ 8871. Weighted counting over disjoint sets of multiples already appears in Chen's paper. The contribution of this note is the family of weights, which depend on gcd(m,6), and the exact certificate. The search was bounded and not exhaustive: one citation index was rate-limited and could not be used, another did not resolve Chen's 1996 paper, so its forward citations could not be traced there, and the community chat archive was searched only in part.

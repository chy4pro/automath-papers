Version 2, 13 October 2026; supersedes version 1.

For a positive integer n, let ρ(n) be the maximum of ∑a∈A 1/a over sets A ⊆ {1,…,n} such that lcm(a,b) > n for distinct members. This version proves, for every integer n ≥ 1, all three inequalities:

  • ρ(n) ≤ 1.00189053 + 4162.91/n, with modulus 420 and exact pre-rounding coefficient K = 2081454577/500000;
  • ρ(n) ≤ 1.00271168 + 638.004/n, with modulus 60 and K = 127600737/200000;
  • ρ(n) ≤ 1.00447967 + 133.853/n, with modulus 12 and K = 2091444/15625.

The proof uses nonnegative weights on pairwise disjoint sets of multiples. Three arrays of rational weights are certified by directed integer finite checks and an Euler–Maclaurin tail estimate with an explicit remainder. The corrected modulus-420 tail values are Ψ1 = 3518369257/52500000 and R1 = 232154535249/50000; the sufficient onset remains 70483. The conjecture ρ(n) ≤ 1 + o(1) is not proved, and no general limit-LP theorem is claimed.

Comparison. As an improvement of the asymptotic leading constant, this improves the strongest bound we located in a literature search of 12 October 2026, including our own version 1 and Y.-G. Chen, On a problem of P. Erdős, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 62 (1996), 101–114. Weighted counting over disjoint multiple sets already appears in Chen's paper; the contribution here is the specific certificates and their numerical costs. The search was bounded, not exhaustive: a citation index was rate-limited, another did not resolve Chen's 1996 record, and community discussion archives were only partly searchable. No priority claim is made.

Version 1 proves B0(n) = 1.0087578638 + 87.5686331624/n as an upper bound. The new displayed bounds improve B0 precisely for n ≥ 10819 (modulus 12), n ≥ 91039 (modulus 60), and n ≥ 593439 (modulus 420). The smallest of the four displayed bounds is version 1 through n = 10818, modulus 12 for 10819–285154, modulus 60 for 285155–4292645, and modulus 420 from 4292646 onward. The paper supplies exact rational crossover calculations and comparisons with Chen's finite-n bound.

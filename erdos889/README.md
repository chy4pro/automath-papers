# An effective finiteness theorem for an exceptional set of Erdős and Selfridge

Zenodo: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22962744 (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22962743), published 2026-09-25.

Erdős Problem #889. Read `main.pdf` (9 pages).

## The claim, in two sentences

For $n\ge 0$, $k\ge 1$ let $v(n,k)$ be the number of primes $p>k$ dividing $n+k$, and
$v_1(n)=\sup_{k\ge1}v(n,k)$; Erdős and Selfridge (1967) could not prove that $\{n:v_1(n)=1\}$ is
finite. This note proves it with an explicit bound: every integer $n\ge N_0$, where
$\log N_0=e^{45.28}\approx4.62\times10^{19}$, has some $k\le10\log n$ with $v(n,k)\ge2$, and the
same method gives an explicit threshold uniform in a second parameter $l$.

## What is not claimed

* The main problem, $v_0(n)\to\infty$, is not addressed.
* No lower bound of $v_0(n)\ge3$ or $v_l(n)\ge3$ is proved.
* The conjecture that $330$ is the largest $n$ with $v_1(n)=1$ (and that $80$ is the largest $n$
  with $V_1(n)=1$) is not settled; $N_0$ is astronomically larger than any $n$ that has been
  checked, so the exceptional set itself is still not determined.
* The qualitative finiteness of $\{n:v_1(n)=1\}$, with an effective but uncomputed bound, is not
  new: it also follows from a 1981 theorem of Langevin together with an elementary lemma (Section
  7.1 of the paper). What is new is the explicit constant $N_0$ and the explicit uniform-in-$l$
  version.

## Reproducing the numbers

The threshold $N_0$ (and the uniform-in-$l$ threshold, the per-$l$ table, and the sharper Remark
7.2 constant) is certified by interval arithmetic, not floating point:

```
python3 problems/erdos889/n0_compute.py            # constants and thresholds, under 1 s
python3 problems/erdos889/n0_compute.py --check     # also the finite check of Lemma R, ~6 s
```

Requires Python 3.12 and mpmath 1.3.0. Every inequality the proof needs is evaluated from interval
endpoints (60 digits, outward rounding) and printed as `OK`/`FAIL`; the script's output is
reproduced in the paper's Appendix A.

The finite witness table (least $k$ with $v(n,k)\ge2$ for every $n\le10^7$, and the exception lists
for $l=1,2,3,5,8,13,20$) is evidence only — nothing in the proof depends on it — and lives in
`problems/erdos889/witness/`, generated by `gen_witness.py` and independently re-checked by
`check_witness.py` (a separate trial-division implementation sharing no code with the generator).
It reproduces the Erdős–Selfridge 1967 list of $29$ exceptional $n\le330$ exactly and finds no
further exception up to $10^7$; the largest least-$k$ found is $11$, at $n=210$ and $n=840$.

Same-vendor review only: an independent Claude referee, working in a fresh context, checked every
step against the primary sources and recomputed the constants independently. Verdict:
**PASS-WITH-REPAIRS** — no mathematical gap was found in Theorem A, Theorem A$_l$, Theorem
A$_{\mathrm{unif}}$, Corollary A1 or Corollary A$_l$; the repairs (R1–R6) concerned wording and
citations only and are incorporated into `main.tex`. No cross-vendor or human referee has checked
the proof, and there is no Lean formalisation (the proof uses Matveev's theorem, which is not
available in Mathlib). Two sources relevant to prior art could not be consulted: Erdős, *Some of
my new and almost new problems and results in combinatorial number theory* (1998), p. 178, and
Shorey & Tijdeman, *Exponential Diophantine Equations* (1986); either may already contain Theorem A
or its qualitative form.
